## Clear Vision, Simplified 

#### **A Patient’s Guide to Selecting Premium Intraocular Lenses (IOLs)** 

When preparing for cataract surgery or Refractive Lens Exchange (RLE), one of the most important choices you and your eye surgeon will make together is selecting your Intraocular Lens (IOL). An IOL is an advanced, clear artificial lens that permanently replaces your eye’s cloudy natural lens. 

# **Understanding Your Lens Options** 

###  **Standard Monofocal IOLs** 

A monofocal lens provides clear vision at **one fixed distance** , usually for distance vision. 

#### **Best suited for patients who:** 

- Don't mind wearing reading glasses 

- Prefer a simple and reliable option 

- Have minimal visual demands 

You may still need spectacles for reading, computer work, or other close tasks. 

###  **Premium IOLs** 

Premium IOLs are designed to reduce dependence on glasses and provide improved visual performance for selected patients. 

Not everyone requires a premium lens, but many patients benefit significantly when the correct lens is chosen. 

# **Which Premium Lens Is Right for You?** 

### **1. Toric IOL** 

If your eye has significant corneal astigmatism (>0.75 Diopters), a Toric IOL can correct it during cataract surgery. 

Pros: 

- Corrects astigmatism during cataract surgery itself 

- Reduces dependence on cylindrical glasses post-op 

- Can be incorporated along with other Premium IOLs 

Cons: 

- Requires precise intraoperative alignment (degree-specific) 

Ideal if you: 

- ✔ Have blurred vision due to astigmatism 

- ✔ Want clearer distance vision 

- ✔ Wish to reduce dependence on distance glasses 

### **2. Multifocal and Trifocal IOL** 

These lenses provide vision at multiple distances. 

They help patients see: 

- Far 

- Intermediate 

- Near 

Pros: 

- Highest chance of complete glasses independence 

- Excellent near vision for reading and detailed work 

- Strong intermediate vision for digital screens 

Cons: 

- Higher risk of glare, halos, and starbursts at night 

- Slightly reduced contrast sensitivity in low light 

- 

- Requires healthy retina and cornea for best results 

Ideal if you: 

- ✔ Read books frequently 

- ✔ Use smartphones 

- ✔ Enjoy travelling 

- ✔ Want minimal dependence on spectacles 

Things to know 

Some patients notice halos or glare around lights at night, especially during the initial weeks after surgery. Most people adapt well over time. 

### **3. Extended Depth of Focus (EDOF) IOL** 

EDOF lenses provide a continuous range of vision with excellent intermediate vision. 

Pros: 

- Minimal glare and halos — excellent for night driving 

- Superior intermediate vision (computer, dashboard, kitchen work) 

- Faster neuroadaptation (brain adjusts quicker) 

Cons: 

- May still require reading glasses for very fine print 

- Premium pricing 

Ideal if you: 

- ✔ Spend long hours on computers 

- ✔ Drive regularly 

- ✔ Want better distance and intermediate vision 

- ✔ Prefer fewer halos than some multifocal lenses 

You may still need reading glasses for very small print. 

# **4. Enhanced Monofocal IOLs** 

Enhanced Monofocals bridge the gap between standard Monofocals and true Premium IOLs. They provide **distance vision plus mild intermediate improvement** without any diffractive optics. 

Pros: 

- Virtually zero glare or halos 

- Excellent contrast sensitivity 

- Suitable for patients with retinal or corneal compromise 

Cons: 

- 

- Requires reading glasses for near work

Recommended: 

**Multifocal IOL** 

I work on computers for several hours 

Recommended: 

#### **EDOF IOL** 

I drive frequently, especially at night 

Recommended: 

#### **Monofocal or EDOF IOL** 

I have astigmatism 

Recommended: **Toric IOL** (or Toric Multifocal/EDOF if appropriate) 

I want maximum freedom from glasses 

Recommended: **Multifocal IOL** , if your eye health is suitable.
